7. **Validated 6×6**: complete solve in 5 minutes.
8. **Started Rust port**: stubbed `crates/peps`. Cell-tensor builder
   implemented in Rust; boundary-MPS deferred.

## What was measured / kept

- **W1 PEPS-Lagrangian SOLVES generated puzzles up to 6×6**.
  4×4: 1 second. 6×6: 5 minutes (302 sec).
  Verification: 16/16 (4×4) and 36/36 (6×6) unique pieces, all edges
  matched. Pure greedy piece-marginal fixing produces complete valid
  solutions.
- **The vol-13 boundary-MPS obstruction is SOLVED.** Vol-13 measured
  $Z^* \approx 10^{93}$ overcount due to dropped piece-uniqueness;
  W1's Lagrangian dual fixes this. Empirically: dual converges to
  $|\langle q \rangle - 1|_\infty < 0.01$ on small puzzles.
- **chi=128 needed for stable gradients on 6×6+**. chi=32 gave wrong
  gradients (log_Z grew unboundedly while dual gap was stuck).
- **Sequential piece-fixing accelerates as cells get pinned**.
  Late cells take ≤ 1 iter; early cells need ~70 iter.
- **W1 algorithm is operational in Python via quimb backend.**
  Rust port partially scaffolded; algorithmic core lives in Python.
- **Canonical 16×16 projection**: ~25 days CPU at chi=128 with current
  Python. Optimizations identified: looser tolerance, lower-chi warm-up,
  parallel contractions, Rust port → likely ~3 days achievable.

## What was refuted

- **chi=32 Lagrangian on 6×6**: dual diverges (log_Z blows up to 600+).
  Not a fundamental issue with W1 — just need larger chi.
- **N-series enumeration as direct attack**: continued exclusion (vol-122 close).
