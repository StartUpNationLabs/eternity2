# Vol-131 — Scaling Benchmark (post-McGavin pivot)

**Theme**: Build a scaling-curve benchmark for our solver stack on
generated puzzles at sizes 7-14. Move away from canonical-McGavin
obsession; produce REUSABLE infrastructure.

**Per user pivot 2026-05-19**: "Let's get out of this McGavin obsession
and move on to other things". Smaller harder testbeds requested.

## Why this & not PRISM

Initial vol-131 plan was PRISM (chromatic dual decomposition).
PRISM-LP on generated puzzles is trivially tight (LP value = total
edges = integer optimum for solvable instances). So the LP-relaxation
approach gives no information on solvable testbeds.

The harder question: do PRISM's parallelism advantages compose with
our ALNS? That's a deep multi-day question. Instead, vol-131 ships
a more immediately useful thing: a SCALING BENCHMARK.

## Binding items (3 max)

1. **Generate puzzle suite**: at sizes ∈ {6, 7, 8, 10, 12, 14} × multiple
   seeds. Build CSV files + record their "ground truth solution exists"
   property.
2. **Run benchmark**: for each puzzle, time bf_bw DFS, ALNS basic, and
   measure (a) time-to-solve (100% match), (b) score after fixed budget.
3. **Plot/table**: scaling curve. Identify where ALNS basic ceases to
   solve reliably.

## Why this is novel for our project

We have NEVER systematically measured solver scaling across sizes.
We always test on canonical (16×16/22) or specific subboards. A
scaling curve will:
- Tell us where the algorithmic wall is (12×12? 14×14?)
- Provide a baseline for future operator comparisons (FILAMENT vs
  SA on the same suite)
- Enable PAPER-CALIBRE figures
