### Tier S (high novelty AND high EV against our 122-vol body of work)

#### W1. Hyperoptimized approximate tensor-network contraction for spin glasses

Source: Gray & Chan, *Phys. Rev. X* 14, 011009 (2024); follow-up arXiv:2407.21287
applied to rugged-landscape spin glasses on 2D/3D lattices.

**What is it.** Represent the Boltzmann distribution $Z(\beta) = \sum_x e^{-\beta E(x)}$
as a tensor network on the lattice. Approximately contract it with bond-dimension
$\chi$ to recover *marginal probability distributions per site*. Sequentially:
compute the marginal at site $i$, project to most-probable state, fix it,
move to next site. **Independent of landscape ruggedness** — cost depends on
$\chi$ and topology, not on the energy landscape.

**Why genuinely new for E2.**
- Boundary-MPS (vol-13 refuted) used a 1D MPS for *counting* the partition
  function. W1 uses a 2D PEPS (or PEPS-like) for *single-site marginals*.
  Completely different operational primitive.
- It doesn't traverse the landscape (no MCMC, no ALNS). Cost is N² for 2D
  short-range with fixed $\chi$.
- 2304-spin 2D lattice (48×48 spin glass) was their benchmark — comparable
  scale to our 256-piece × 4-rotation = 1024-variable lattice.

**Adaptation challenge.**
- Spin glass has 2-state variables and pairwise interactions on a lattice.
  E2 has 1024-state variables (piece × rotation) and binary "match" interactions.
- Two routes:
  - **(a) Direct.** Build a tensor at each cell with mode = piece-rotation (1024
    states), bonds with neighbors via 23-color matching. χ would need to be
    >> 23 → expensive but potentially tractable.
  - **(b) Color-as-variable.** Each *edge* has 23 colors; one variable per edge,
    constraint that 4 edges meeting at a cell must come from a valid piece.
    This is the proper PEPS encoding: tensors at each cell encode the puzzle's
    constraint via piece-validity counts. ~580 edges total — smaller variable
    space.

**Concrete first step.** Implement (b) on a 6×6 generated puzzle first. Use
`opt_einsum` + `cotengra` (Python). If χ=16 gives high-quality marginals on
6×6, scale to 8×8, 12×12, 16×16. Compare against `solver-engine` joe profile
on the same instances.

**EV.** **Very high.** It's a continuous-but-globally-rigorous method that
hasn't been tried at canonical scale. The 2024 paper's "low-energy
configuration" extraction is exactly what we need for high-matched-edge boards.

**Effort.** 1 week for 6×6 PoC; 1-2 more weeks for canonical scale.

**Linked**: [[boundary-mps]] (refuted earlier 1D variant), [[k11-cross-domain-brainstorm]].

#### W2. Survey Propagation (Mézard-Zecchina) at canonical scale

Source: Braunstein-Mézard-Zecchina 2002/2005; backtracking-SP 2016 Nature Comms;
GPU implementation 2006+ extensions.

**What is it.** A generalization of belief propagation that passes **surveys
over message clusters** rather than messages. Designed for k-SAT close to the
SAT-UNSAT threshold where BP cycles. Crucially: backtracking-SP can find
solutions "very close to the threshold, in a region unreachable by any other
algorithm" (per the 2016 Nature Comms paper).

**Why genuinely new for E2.**
- We measured BP on E2 (vol-11/12) — 18.84% interior reduction; not strong
  enough alone. But BP and SP are different algorithms; SP captures
  *cluster structure* that BP cannot.
- E2 has **strong cluster structure** in solution space (vols 18-22, 99-115):
  basin level sets are σ-orbits, mismatch-zlib clusters, etc. SP was designed
  for exactly this regime.
- The 2016 backtracking-SP paper showed SP can reach the algorithmically-hard
  threshold region. E2's 469-480 gap may well be in this regime.

**Adaptation.**
- E2's factor graph is small by SP standards (256 piece-vars × 1024 piece-rot
  states, plus edge-color constraints). The Braunstein-Mézard implementation
  on a 16×16 lattice is well within SP's tested scale.
- Encode as factor graph: variable = (cell, piece-rotation); factor = color
  match constraint per edge.

**Concrete first step.** Use `Braunstein-Mézard`'s public C code (or thibsej's
github implementation) on a 6×6 E2 encoded as k-SAT (we already have a
SAT encoder, `eternity2-sat-encoder`). If SP converges and gives a marginal,
adapt for 8×8, then 16×16.

**EV.** **High.** SP is a *physics-motivated* algorithm validated on the
hardest known SAT instances. We haven't tried it.

**Effort.** ~1 week.

**Linked**: [[bp-marginals]] (BP measurement), [[edge-bp-measurement]].

### Tier A (high novelty, moderate EV)

#### W3. Kovalsky-Glasner-Basri 2014 — Vandermonde-LP convex relaxation

Source: Kovalsky, Glasner, Basri. "A Global Approach for Solving Edge-Matching
Puzzles." *SIAM J. Imaging Sciences* 2015 (also arXiv:1409.5957).

**What is it.**
- Reformulate puzzle as polynomial system using exponential change of
  variables: $T_i = e^{t_i}$.
- Constraint "color $c$ appears at orientation $\theta$ exactly $K(c,\theta)$ times"
  becomes a polynomial in $T_i$'s.
- **Vandermonde relaxation**: assignment matrix $P$ permutation. Replace
  permutation by **doubly stochastic matrix** (Birkhoff-von Neumann polytope).
  LP feasibility on a Vandermonde-structured matrix.
- **SDP rank-one alternative**: for general (no preset locations) case.

**Why genuinely new for E2.**
- Vault has Birkhoff-von Neumann mention (vol-54) but never built the
  Vandermonde-Vandermonde-LP iterative method.
- They solved 6×6 (2 iterations), 8×8 (6 iterations), and tangram. Never
  scaled to 16×16 in the paper.
- **Different convex relaxation than vol-44 cell-pair LP**. Vol-44 LP-UB
  doesn't use the Vandermonde algebraic structure — it's a generic LP on
  variable-edge incidence. Kovalsky uses the exponential algebraic structure
  with polynomial closure.

**Adaptation.**
- Need to set up the polynomial system: $\sum_{i,j} s_{i,j}(c,\theta) \cdot f(t_i + b_{i,j}) = 0$
  for each (color, orientation) pair.
- Then iterative LP: $P^{n+1} = \arg\max \langle P^n, P \rangle$ subject to
  doubly-stochastic + linear constraints.

**Concrete first step.** Build a 4×4 PoC in Julia (paper has Julia heritage)
or Python+`scipy.optimize.linprog`. If it solves 4×4 in <100 iterations,
scale to 8×8 and 16×16.

**EV.** Medium-high. The algorithm scales sublinearly in iterations on their
benchmarks. If we get even partial completion, the partial structure may seed
ALNS.

**Effort.** ~1 week for PoC; ~2 weeks for canonical scale.

**Linked**: [[y-linearisation-cell-fractional-gap]] (Birkhoff mention),
[[publishability]] (mentions Kovalsky paper but never built).

#### W4. LKH-style generalized k-opt for piece permutation

Source: Helsgaun, "General k-opt submoves for the Lin-Kernighan TSP heuristic."
Math. Prog. Comp. (2009).

**What is it.**
- LKH (Lin-Kernighan-Helsgaun) is the state-of-the-art TSP heuristic.
- Key insight: instead of fixed-k k-opt (e.g., 2-opt or 3-opt), use *general*
  k-opt where k is chosen dynamically by following a sequence of edge-swap
  candidates that maintain a tour.
- Each "move" is a *sequential edge-chain* — start an edge swap, evaluate
  the gain, decide whether to extend the chain or close it.

**Why genuinely new for E2.**
- Our ALNS operators (basic, winning5, etc.) are FIXED-CARDINALITY destroys
  (1-cell rotate, 2-cell swap, k-cell band). LKH's *adaptive-cardinality
  edge-chain* is fundamentally different.
- We documented (vol-20, vol-65, vol-99) that E2 basins are operator-locked
  under K≤5. LKH's chains routinely reach K=10-20 by chaining gains.
- **Crucial**: E2 has piece-permutation structure on a 16×16 *grid* (not a
  tour), so it's not literal LKH. But the *chain* methodology — start a swap,
  test gain, conditionally chain — applies to any permutation problem.

**Adaptation.**
- Define a "chain": start with swap (pos_a, pos_b). Compute Δ edges. If Δ<0,
  identify the WORST piece in {a,b} (= the one whose new neighbors mismatch
  most). Swap it with another piece. Repeat until Δ≥0 OR chain length > K_max.
- **Equivalent to a one-elligible-move random walk with score-conditioned
  termination.**

**Concrete first step.** Implement `lkh_chain_e2` as a new operator alongside
`worst_band` in `alns_e2`. Apply to a basin like 458 strict-canonical or 459.
Compare lifts.

**EV.** Medium-high. We've literally never tried adaptive-cardinality moves.

**Effort.** 2-3 days for PoC.

**Linked**: [[alns]], [[basin-rigidity-refutation]].

### Tier B (moderate novelty, speculative)

#### W5. GFlowNet-amortized ant-colony sampling (GFACS)

Source: Kim et al., "Ant Colony Sampling with GFlowNets for Combinatorial
Optimization." AISTATS 2025.

**What is it.** Train a GFlowNet to amortize a prior over combinatorial
solutions; iteratively refine via ACO-style stochastic search. The GFlowNet
samples *diverse high-reward* solutions; the ACO updates reward shaping.

**Why genuinely new.** Our portfolio uses backtracking + ALNS + Blackwood.
GFlowNet *generates* solutions via amortized sampling — fundamentally
different generative paradigm. Diverse-by-construction.

**Adaptation.** Define state = partial board, action = place piece, reward =
matched edges. Train GFlowNet via flow-matching loss on synthetic 6×6/8×8
puzzles, transfer to canonical.

**EV.** Medium. RL/ML approaches have been speculated (D4) but never
shipped at scale.

**Effort.** 2-3 weeks (ML training + integration).

#### W6. Discrete score-based diffusion (SEDD, DeFoG)

Source: SEDD (Lou et al. 2024), DeFoG (2025).

**What is it.** Discrete diffusion with score entropy loss. Generate
combinatorial structures (graphs, sequences) via reverse diffusion.

**Why for E2.** A board is a discrete 16×16 grid with piece-rotation labels.
Discrete diffusion can generate "valid" boards via a learned score. If
trained on solved-near-complete partials, may generate near-complete
hypotheses.

**EV.** Low-medium. Speculative. Training cost high. Generative-fidelity for
1024-state discrete labels untested.

**Effort.** 3-4 weeks.

#### W7. Frozen-variable / backbone analysis at canonical scale

Source: Frozen-variable theory in random CSPs (Achlioptas, Coja-Oghlan,
2007-2012); freezing threshold for graph coloring (Molloy 2012).

**What is it.** For random k-SAT/k-coloring beyond the clustering threshold,
solutions cluster and inside each cluster most variables are *frozen* (take
the same value in all solutions of the cluster). Algorithmic barrier = frozen
threshold.

**Why for E2.** We have empirical backbone measurements (vol-17 found
17/18-cell backbone) but vol-20 corrected this to just 5 hint cells. **Have
we measured the freezing structure of E2's 459-class basins?**

Concrete: Pick a 459 basin. For each cell, count the number of distinct
piece-rotation values across all 459+ boards in that basin. Cells with
exactly 1 piece-rotation across all boards = frozen. Cells with 4+ values =
liquid. Map the freezing fraction by region.

**EV.** Medium. The measurement itself gives a *new* structural signature for
basins.

**Effort.** 1-2 days.

#### W8. Tropical-geometry framework rigidity (2025)

Source: arXiv:2502.10255 "A tropical approach to rigidity: counting
realisations of frameworks."

**What is it.** Tropical geometry on graphs to count distinct realizations
(rigid embeddings) of a framework. Recursive deletion-contraction on a
bigraph.

**Why for E2.** A solved board is a "framework" — a graph with constraints
(color matches). Tropical count of realizations could give a closed-form upper
bound on the number of distinct full-score boards.

**EV.** Low-medium (speculative; tropical methods on E2 untested).

**Effort.** 1-2 weeks reading + framework adaptation.

## Picking order

For the next autonomous push, I'd pick **W1 (tensor-network)** first because:
1. It's algorithmically clean (single iterative procedure, well-defined cost).
2. The 2024 paper validates it on rugged spin glasses — exactly the regime
   we've struggled in.
3. It doesn't require ML training; just `cotengra` / `opt_einsum`.
4. If it gives high-quality marginals, those marginals seed CSP/ALNS like
   BP did (vol-14) but much stronger.

Then **W2 (Survey Propagation)** — also clean, also physics-motivated,
implementation exists in public form.

Then **W3 (Kovalsky Vandermonde-LP)** — novel relaxation never tried on E2.

W4 (LKH-chain) is the cheapest unrelated win (2-3 days).
