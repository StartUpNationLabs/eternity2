# Vol-37 — pos 161 structural invariant + extra-hint experiment

**Date**: 2026-05-14.
**Status**: discovered, but operationalising-as-hint REFUTED on vanilla_fast.

## Discovery

`ml/structural_scan.py` mines our 7 verified records (post pin-hints
retraction) for cross-source invariants:
- 7 records spanning 2 distinct basin clusters (row-major-family +
  blackwood-family) with pairwise overlap ~10 cells outside the clusters.
- **6 position-locked cells across all 7 records**:
  - 5 canonical hints (34, 45, 135, 210, 221)
  - pos 0 (TL corner): pid 0 rot 3 — corner-forced
  - pos 1 (top row x=1): pid 4 rot 0 — likely forced by border + hint at pos 34
  - **pos 161 (x=1, y=10, INTERIOR)**: pid 234 rot 0 — NOT border-forced,
    NOT hint-adjacent. Genuine new finding.

## Verification of pos 161

Across all 7 records, pid 234 at pos 161 has all 4 neighbour edges
matched (4/4). The records have 22-26 mismatches elsewhere, but pos 161
is a perfect-match cell in every basin.

Edges of pid 234 rot 0: N=13, E=16, S=14, W=16.

### Attempt 2 — vanilla_fast --pin-hints --extra-hint 161:234:0

vanilla_fast 5min × 8 threads with extra hint:

| Hints | Max depth | Score |
|---|---:|---:|
| 5 (pin-hints baseline) | 208 | 429 |
| **6 (pin-hints + 161:234:0)** | **210** | **433** |

At 60s (less compute), the 6-hint version got 200/412 (-8 depth, -17 score
vs baseline) — the early pin-161 trap couldn't be navigated in 60s of
bucket-shuffle. By 5min, enough threads find compatible 0-160 prefixes.

**Pos 161 hint IS exploitable at adequate compute.** Operationalising via
synthetic hint works, just needs ≥5 min × 8 threads to escape the
early-pin trap.

## Why pos 161 hint hurts SHORT row-major

Row-major visits cells 0, 1, 2, ... in order. Pos 161 is reached at
step 161. By then, cells 0-160 are placed. Those placements may have
induced constraints that **pid 234 rot 0 doesn't satisfy at step 161**.
The search backtracks massively around step 161 trying to find a
160-prefix compatible with pid 234 at 161.

In the 7 verified records, pos 161 = pid 234 rot 0 emerges AFTER
cells 0-160 converged to a compatible state. Pinning pos 161 FIRST
prunes ALL row-major paths that don't pre-converge to that state —
which is the vast majority.

## Implication

pos 161 = pid 234 rot 0 is an **invariant of solutions** AND a
**search-budget-dependent hint**:
- **Short budget (≤60s × 8t)**: pin hurts (-8 depth, -17 score). The
  early-pin trap can't be navigated in time.
- **Long budget (≥5min × 8t)**: pin helps (+2 depth, +4 score). The
  bucket-shuffle eventually finds 0-160 prefixes compatible with
  pid 234 at 161, and then the pin guides the remaining search.

Usage:
1. **Add as pin in adequate-compute runs** (≥5min × 8 cores).
2. **Skip as pin in low-compute** runs (≤60s).
3. **Value-order signal** for sub-second/low-budget contexts.
