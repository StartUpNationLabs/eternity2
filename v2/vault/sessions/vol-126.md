## Math

Difference map: given two sets $A, B$ with projections $P_A, P_B$,
iterate

$$
x_{n+1} = x_n + \beta \bigl[ P_A\bigl(2 P_B(x_n) - x_n\bigr) - P_B(x_n) \bigr].
$$

Fixed point ⇔ $P_A(P_B(x)) = P_B(x) = x_{\text{sol}}$ (a point in
$A \cap B$).

For E2:

- **Set $A$ (cell-valid)**: $x$ assigns to each cell a *distribution*
  over (piece, rotation). $P_A(x)$ is the projection onto valid
  one-hot per cell (argmax + zero rest, or softer renormalisation).
- **Set $B$ (piece-unique + edge-consistent)**: $P_B(x)$ projects to
  the nearest point where (a) each piece is used at most once and (b)
  each shared edge agrees. The piece-uniqueness sub-projection is a
  bipartite assignment problem (LAP); the edge-consistency
  sub-projection couples neighbouring cells. We may iterate these
  two sub-projections inside $P_B$ to convergence.

The state $x$ is in $\mathbb{R}^{256 \times 256 \times 4}$ (cell ×
piece × rot) = 262 144 real numbers. Manageable in memory.

## Risks

- $P_B$ is itself a coupled optimization (LAP + edge-consistency).
  Cheap LAP via Hungarian/JV is $O(N^3)$ on $N = 256$ pieces ≈ 16M
  ops — fast enough per step.
- $\beta$ tuning. Elser's papers suggest $\beta \in [0.5, 1.0]$.
- Convergence not theoretically guaranteed for non-convex constraint
  sets; in practice, difference map has cracked Sudoku, sphere
  packing, protein folding. E2 is structurally similar (combinatorial
  constraint satisfaction). Try and measure.
