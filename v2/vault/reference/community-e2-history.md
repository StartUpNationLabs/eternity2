### What "canonical" means and the 480 false positives

The TOMY-released $2M puzzle is the **canonical 5-hint variant**: 256 specific pieces, 5 pre-pinned positions (the official clues), 16×16 framed grid, 22 colors total (17 interior + 5 border + 1 grey perimeter).

Several boards in the public archives appear to be 480/480 but are NOT canonical:
- **Brendan Owen's Clue-1 set** (and Clue-2): smaller piece sets generated by Brendan with different colors. Some have known 480 solutions; these don't transfer to canonical.
- **TopCoder unframed E2**: removes the border constraint. Different piece set.
- **17-color expansion variant**: pieces have one extra color slot.
- **Mixed Clue1+Clue2 boards** (McGavin 2023-10-09): use 256 pieces drawn from multiple sets.

See `reference_e2_corpus_480_false_positives` memory and [[basin-mcgavin-469]] §Confounds.

## The five key community algorithms

### 1. Selby-Riordan generator

The original generator that produced canonical E2. Mathematical foundation: **expected ~1 solution by design**.

Brendan Owen + Günter stertenbrink derived in 2007 ([from-scratch formula](../docs/community-mining/02_Design_the_hardest_thread.md)):
- Interior color count `I = (196! × 4^196)^(1/392) = 17.14 → 17`
- Border color count `B = (56! × 4! / 17^28)^(1/60) = 4.97 → 5`

The 17/5 split is **chosen exactly** to put the puzzle at the unique-solution boundary. Mathematically rigorous derivation; not folklore.

Carles Mateu (2012, [Springer paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10601-012-9128-9)) later confirmed this is also the **SAT phase-transition peak** for GEMP-F (framed Generalized Edge-Matching Puzzles).

### 2. Verhaard 2008 — set-composition swap-annealing

Louis Verhaard's 467 (2008-09): "set-composition swap-annealing". Documented in [[verhaard-set-sa]]. Key insight: anneal on the composition of the piece sets between regions, not just on positions.

### 3. Blackwood 2020 — heuristic-schedule + break-index

The dominant algorithm of the modern era (469/470 records). See [[blackwood-algorithm]] and [docs/community-mining/09_Blackwood_solver_thread.md](../docs/community-mining/09_Blackwood_solver_thread.md).

**Algorithm (canonical 469 parameters, msg #31, 2020-11-22):**
```
heuristic_sides       = [17, 2, 18]
break_indexes_allowed = [201, 206, 211, 216, 221, 225, 229, 233, 237, 239, 241, 256]
heuristic_array       = piecewise-linear color-exhaustion target
                        index 0 → exhaust 0 of 122
                        index 16 → exhaust 0
                        index 26 → exhaust 28
                        index 56 → exhaust 71
                        index 76 → exhaust 89
                        index 102 → exhaust 106
                        index 160 → exhaust 119 of 122
max_heuristic_index   = 160
```

The 122 = total occurrences of colors {17, 2, 18} across 256 pieces.

**Stopping criterion**: 50 billion iterations before restart ("arbitrary; tried bigger numbers, didn't help much").

**Variants Blackwood reported as not helping**: eliminating 4 sides early instead of 3 (similar), saving sides for end (worse), different heuristics (~2× marginal), SAT solvers (didn't help), GPU (no improvement), pre-solved 2×2 cache (no improvement).

**Bucas's libblackwood**: C with Python-generated unrolled per-cell goto code. **~2.3× faster** than Blackwood's Mono C# (59.4s vs 136.7s on a 469 attempt). 0.09% L1-cache miss rate vs 0.33%. GitHub: `github.com/jfbucas/libblackwood`.

### 4. McGavin engine — 295 M nps backtracker

Peter McGavin's C backtracker (msg #18 of the [Joe pruning thread](../docs/community-mining/05_Joe_pruning_method_thread.md), Jan 2026 ZIP):
- **295 M tile placements/sec single-core**
- 4× faster than typical community engines (~70-90 M/sec)
- Techniques: `gcc -O6 -mcpu=native -march=native`, PGO, sometimes `-m32`, auto-generated per-cell goto-style unrolled code, `fit_table` precomputation.

This is the target [[mcgavin-engine]] for our blackwood-fast comparison. Our current 85 M nps is ~28% of McGavin's throughput.

### 5. Joe pruning policy (2026-01)

**Concrete and recent.** Joe's [pruning-back policy](../docs/community-mining/05_Joe_pruning_method_thread.md) on scan-row backtrackers:
- 99% of backtrack time is at depth > 132
- 70% of backtrack time is at depth > 150
- **Policy**: if N iterations spent at depth > T without progress, prune back to T and restart.

Empirical sweep (Joe's E2-like with 116-100 hints):
- 116 hints, prune-after 1600 → prune-to 150: **49% iteration reduction**
- 100 hints, prune-after 1600 → prune-to 150: **17% reduction** (still useful at low-hint regimes)

Implemented in our [[prune-restart]] (vol-23, our adaptation).

## Standing community wisdom (cross-referenced)

### What works on E2

1. **Heavily optimized scan-row backtrackers** with custom-unrolled per-cell code (McGavin, Bucas).
2. **Bottom-left start, left-right rows** scan order (McGavin 2017 + complex theory).
3. **Blackwood-class heuristic schedule + break-index** for high-score (≥ 467) records.
4. **Joe's prune-back-to-depth policy** (modern, 17-49% iteration reduction).
5. **Post-processing piece-swap improvements** (Carlos Fernandez's "exchange piece").

### What doesn't work on E2

1. **Pure SAT solvers**: cap at **10×10** (verified by Vlasta, gammel's friend's entire university department, Heule + jgallicc 2008-2023).
2. **Brute-force border enumeration**: 10^37 borders, ~10^-33 each extends → wasted work.
3. **Interior-only solving** (frame-first 14×14): 10^58 interior solutions; intractable per stertenbrink 2007 + Brendan 2008.
4. **Quantum**: today's hardware (156 qubits heavy-hex, 120 superconducting) is **>3000 qubits short** of feasible E2 set-cover encoding.
5. **Generic edge-matching propagators** alone — without the Blackwood-class heuristic schedule.

### The 14×14 inner puzzle (recurring topic)

Discussed in multiple threads ([interior rectangles 2008](../docs/community-mining/04_Interior_Rectangles_thread.md), [14×14 thread 2022](../docs/community-mining/11_Inner_14x14_thread.md)):
- Brendan 2008: 4.4×10^13 days at random search.
- Empirically intractable after 19 years (onesmallstep 2025).
- Al Hopfer's NS-1 multiset-equality propagator (2022): boundary parity matters; with correct parity, 14×14 solves in minutes.

## TOMY disclosure (2007 leaflet, re-quoted 2011)

From [docs/community-mining/12_Solution_secrecy_thread.md](../docs/community-mining/12_Solution_secrecy_thread.md):

1. **The generator computer was destroyed** after the solution + clue positions were printed. No digital record kept.
2. **The solution was printed between random text pages** during the prize-locking ceremony.
3. **Neither the inventor nor designers know the solution** (only the generator algorithm).
4. **Court-revealable to a presiding judge** as proof of solvability; otherwise sealed.

Implication: **the sealed Tomy solution might not even match the printed pieces** (Walter Schreur's concern). Mathematical solvability of the printed set is what matters; the sealed answer is irrelevant to community work.
