**ZERO strict-canonical partials in 5000 runs.**

Best score with any hint compliance: 431 at 1/5 hints.

## Why

The Blackwood schedule used by `bf_bw` allows "break-indexes" — positions where the algorithm relaxes the heuristic-side constraint. At certain depths in the search, the hint pieces are evicted by the schedule's exploration. This is fundamental to how Blackwood reaches high depth/score: it sacrifices hint pinning for piece flexibility.

`bf_bw_hinted` exists as a hint-preserving variant, but at 30s budget reaches only depth 208, score 377 — significantly below the matched-edges record (463) AND below the strict-canonical record (459).

## What was refuted

The hypothesis "5000 raw bf_bw runs with offset diversity will yield SOME 5/5-hint partials by chance, which can then be post-filtered" — refuted at p < 0.0002 (0/5000).

The Blackwood schedule's break-index behavior makes hint-preservation impossible in raw bf_bw, regardless of offset.

## What this means for the strict-canonical track

For strict-canonical records (5/5 hints), the viable pipelines are:

1. **`bf_bw_hinted` (hint-preserving raw DFS)**: plateaus at ~377-400. Not competitive with our 458 strict record.
2. **`bf_bw_hinted` + ALNS basic post-step**: the vol-122 recipe that produced 458 strict. ALNS *with hint preservation* (post-vol-14 fix) lifts ~+3 to +5 over the raw partial. But user excluded ALNS.
3. **Pre-existing 5/5-hint 457/458/459 DB bases as starting points** (the recipe I outlined earlier): also requires ALNS lifts.

**Without ALNS**, the strict-canonical ceiling reachable from raw DFS is ~400. The 459 strict in DB exists because of ALNS, not raw DFS.
